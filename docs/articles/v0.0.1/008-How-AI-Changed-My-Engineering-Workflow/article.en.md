# How AI Changed My Engineering Workflow?

[Medium](https://tareknajem04.medium.com/ai-assisted-professional-engineering-with-net-342dd2a3f965)
[LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-assisted-professional-engineering-withnet-tarek-najem-2udwe)

When people hear that this project was built with the help of AI, they often make the same assumption.

"AI wrote the project."

That assumption couldn't be further from reality.

The biggest contribution AI made was not writing code.

It was improving how I think about engineering.

Throughout this project, AI became a conversation partner.

We discussed architecture.

We challenged design decisions.

We compared alternatives.

We reorganized documentation.

We refined publishing workflows.

Sometimes we spent hours discussing a single engineering decision before writing a single line of code.

That was the real value.

AI dramatically reduced the cost of exploration.

Questions that previously required hours of research could now be explored through structured engineering conversations.

Different architectural options could be compared in minutes.

Documentation could evolve together with the software instead of becoming an afterthought.

Perhaps the most surprising outcome was that writing became a design activity.

Every article clarified the architecture.

Every architectural decision improved the documentation.

Every discussion generated knowledge worth preserving.

The project gradually evolved into a dialogue between engineering experience and artificial intelligence.

That dialogue never removed responsibility.

Every important decision remained mine.

Every architectural trade-off required human judgment.

Every compromise reflected the project's long-term vision.

AI accelerated thinking.

It did not replace it.

Looking back, I don't believe AI made me a faster programmer.

I believe it made me a more disciplined engineer.

It encouraged documenting decisions instead of relying on memory.

It rewarded structured thinking.

It made iteration inexpensive.

Most importantly, it transformed engineering from a sequence of isolated implementation tasks into an ongoing design conversation.

That may be the most important lesson I've learned.

The future of software engineering is unlikely to be humans competing with AI.

It is far more likely to be humans learning how to engineer better by thinking together with AI.

Perhaps that is the real meaning of AI-assisted professional engineering.
